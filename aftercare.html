Healing Guide

Tattoo Aftercare

The next two to four weeks decide how your tattoo looks for the rest of your life. Follow these steps and it'll heal crisp, bold and exactly as designed.

The First Few Days

  1. Leave the wrap on for as long as your artist told you — usually a few hours for cling wrap, or 3–5 days for a clear healing film (second skin). If fluid builds up under the film or it starts lifting, take it off early.
  2. Wash gently with clean, lukewarm water and a mild, fragrance‑free soap, using clean hands only — no cloths or sponges. Remove any plasma or excess ink, but don't scrub.
  3. Pat dry with a clean paper towel and let it air‑dry for a few minutes.
  4. Moisturise lightly with a thin layer of the aftercare balm we recommend, or an unscented lotion, 2–3 times a day. Your tattoo should look soft, not shiny or greasy.
  5. Repeat washing and moisturising 2–3 times a day until the peeling stage is done.

Do

  • Wear loose, clean, breathable clothing over the area.
  • Sleep on clean bedding and try not to lie on the tattoo.
  • Let flaking skin fall off by itself.
  • Keep drinking water and look after yourself — healthy skin heals better.
  • Use a high‑SPF sunscreen on the tattoo for life, once it's fully healed.

Don't

  • Pick, scratch or peel — it pulls ink out and can scar.
  • Swim in the sea, pools, rivers or hot tubs for at least 2–3 weeks.
  • Sunbathe or sit on the beach with the tattoo exposed while it heals.
  • Soak it in the bath. Short showers are fine.
  • Use Vaseline, scented creams or heavy ointments.

Living on the South Coast?

Sea water, sand and strong KZN sun are the biggest threats to a healing tattoo. Plan your session so you're not due at the beach, a pool party or a fishing trip for the next three weeks — it's the single best thing you can do for your ink.

What's Normal — and What Isn't

For the first few days some redness, swelling, tenderness and a little weeping of clear fluid or ink is normal. Around days 5–14 the tattoo will itch, flake and may look cloudy or dull — this is the skin healing, and the colour comes back once it settles.

See a doctor if redness or swelling spreads or gets worse after day three, the area gets hot or very painful, there's thick yellow or green discharge, a bad smell, red streaks, or you get a fever. Then let us know too.
